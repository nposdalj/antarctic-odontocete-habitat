# 2.2 Deep oceanography — methods draft

*Drafted from `02_deep_oceanography/deep_oceanography.R`, `layer_boundaries.R` and `config.R`. Flags: `[INSERT]` = information not in the code; `[CONFIRM]` = value to verify. Revised 2026-11-19 for the depth layers.*

---

To describe the water column at each site, daily potential temperature and practical salinity were taken from the Copernicus Marine Service global ocean physics reanalysis (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030, dataset version 202311; 1/12°, daily means), and daily dissolved oxygen was taken from the companion global biogeochemistry hindcast (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_BGC_001_029, dataset version 202406; 1/4°, daily means), both accessed 17–18 November 2026 (Section 2.5). Each variable was averaged over all ocean grid cells within a ±20 km box centered on each site, at every model level from the surface (0.5 m) to 1000 m. Because the shelf slope runs through each box, only part of the box reaches the deeper levels, so deep means represent only the cells deep enough to contain those levels. Oxygen was unavailable below the deepest water in the coarser biogeochemistry box (773 m at KGI, 1046 m at CI).

We divided the upper 1000 m into three layers, used both to describe the water column and to define the habitat predictors (Section 2.6): a surface layer (0–100 m), a mid-water layer (100–500 m), and a deep layer (500–1000 m). The boundaries were derived from the reanalysis at all three sites using two independent lines of evidence (Supplement S[INSERT]; Fig. S[INSERT]). First, depths were grouped by how similarly their daily temperature–salinity anomalies varied over the 10-year record, using depth-constrained clustering (CONISS; Grimm 1987). Second, we identified water-mass features in the 10-year mean profiles: the deepest winter mixed layer, the Winter Water temperature minimum, the main pycnocline, and the warm-water core of Circumpolar Deep Water. The surface-layer base (100 m) lies below the deepest winter mixed layer and the Winter Water minimum (51–92 m) and coincides with the coherence breaks at EI and KGI (102–118 m). The mid-water layer contains the warm Circumpolar Deep Water core (temperature maximum at 266–454 m), and its base (500 m) lies near the coherence breaks below that core at EI and CI (462–502 m; 692 m at KGI). The deep layer was capped at 1000 m because, below ~1060 m, less than half of the KGI and CI boxes contains water.

Daily profiles were linearly interpolated onto a regular grid in the square root of depth (300 levels, no extrapolation) and displayed as one continuous depth–time section from January 2014 to December 2016 (Fig. 2). Each period used the box of the site that was recording, or about to record, in that period: EI for 2014, KGI for January 2015 – January 2016, and CI for February – December 2016. Sea-ice concentration from the same reanalysis and box (Section 2.3) was shown above the sections. Layer-mean temperature, salinity, oxygen and eddy kinetic energy were summarized as the mean ± SD over each site's recording period (Table S[INSERT]). Temperature–salinity diagrams for each site's recording period were plotted with contours of potential density anomaly (σθ) calculated at zero pressure using the UNESCO EOS-80 equation of state (oce R package 1.8-3; Kelley & Richards 2024) (Fig. S1).

**Figure 2 (draft legend; main text).** Daily water-column conditions from January 2014 to December 2016 in the upper 1000 m. The record is assembled from the ±20 km box around EI (2014), KGI (January 2015 – January 2016), and CI (February – December 2016). Thin vertical black lines mark where the source site changes, and colored outlines mark each site's recording period. Top: sea-ice concentration (%). Lower panels: potential temperature (°C), salinity, and dissolved oxygen (mmol m⁻³) from the Copernicus Marine Service global physics reanalysis (temperature, salinity; 1/12°) and biogeochemistry hindcast (oxygen; 1/4°), plotted on a square-root depth axis with white contours (0.5 °C, 0.1, and 20 mmol m⁻³, respectively). Dashed lines mark the boundaries of the surface (0–100 m), mid-water (100–500 m) and deep (500–1000 m) layers; right-hand labels give the species whose foraging depths fall in each layer (Pm, sperm whale; Gm, long-finned pilot whale; Oo, killer whale; BW29, southern bottlenose whale; BW37, BW37 beaked whale). Grey areas mark depths with no model water in the box; at KGI, oxygen is unavailable below 773 m, so the deep-layer oxygen mean there covers 500–773 m.

**Figure S1 (draft legend; supplement).** Temperature–salinity diagrams for EI, KGI, and CI over each site's recording period. Each point is a daily box-mean (±20 km) value at one model level (0.5–1000 m) from the Copernicus Marine Service global physics reanalysis, colored by depth (square-root scale). Grey contours show potential density anomaly (σθ, kg m⁻³; labelled every 0.2 kg m⁻³), and the dashed line shows the surface freezing temperature.

**Figure S[INSERT] (draft legend; supplement, layer boundaries).** Evidence for the depth-layer boundaries at EI, KGI and CI. Left: 10-year mean profiles of potential temperature, salinity, potential density anomaly (σθ) and dissolved oxygen, with water-mass markers (dashed): deepest winter mixed layer (90th percentile of daily mixed layer depth), Winter Water temperature minimum (December–March mean profile), main pycnocline (maximum density gradient below the winter mixed layer), and the warm-water temperature maximum. Solid lines: boundaries of the three most coherent depth groups. Right: correlation between depths of daily temperature–salinity anomalies (seasonal cycle removed) over the 10-year record, with the coherence boundaries (CONISS clustering) and species depths (ticks). The adopted layers are 0–100, 100–500 and 500–1000 m.
