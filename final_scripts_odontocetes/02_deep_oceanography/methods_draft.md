# 2.2 Deep oceanography — methods draft

*Drafted from `02_deep_oceanography/deep_oceanography.R` and `config.R`. Flags: `[INSERT]` = information not in the code; `[CONFIRM]` = value to verify.*

---

To describe the water column at each site, including the depths at which each species is thought to forage, daily potential temperature and practical salinity were taken from the Copernicus Marine Service global ocean physics reanalysis (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030, dataset version 202311; 1/12°, daily means), and daily dissolved oxygen was taken from the companion global biogeochemistry hindcast (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_BGC_001_029, dataset version 202406; 1/4°, daily means), both accessed 17–18 November 2026 (Section 2.5). Each variable was averaged over all ocean grid cells within a ±20 km box centered on each site, at every model level from the surface (0.5 m) to ~1684 m. Because the shelf slope means that only part of each box reaches the deeper levels, deep means represent only the cells deep enough to contain those levels. For each site and product, we identified the shallowest level at which less than 50% of the box contained water: for temperature and salinity this was ~1062 m at KGI and CI and ~1684 m at EI, and for oxygen it was ~773 m at KGI and ~1046 m at CI. Oxygen was unavailable below the deepest water in the coarser biogeochemistry box (~773 m at KGI, ~1046 m at CI).

Daily profiles were linearly interpolated onto a regular grid in the square root of depth (300 levels, no extrapolation) and displayed as one continuous depth–time section from January 2014 to December 2016 (Fig. 2). Each period used the box of the site that was recording, or about to record, in that period: EI for 2014, KGI for January 2015 – January 2016, and CI for February – December 2016. Sea-ice concentration from the same reanalysis and box (Section 2.3) was shown above the sections. Nominal species depths (Table [INSERT]) were marked on the sections. Temperature, salinity, and oxygen at each species depth (nearest model level) were summarized as the mean ± SD over each site's recording period (Table S[INSERT]). Temperature–salinity diagrams for each site's recording period were plotted with contours of potential density anomaly (σθ) calculated at zero pressure using the UNESCO EOS-80 equation of state (oce R package 1.8-3; Kelley & Richards 2024) (Fig. S1). No water-mass classification was applied.

### Notes for Natalie
- **Main vs supplement:** the draft puts the sections in the main text and the T–S diagrams in the supplement, as you suggested. Oxygen is in the sections and in the species-depth table (`ts_at_species_depths.csv`: new columns `o2_model_depth_m`, `o2_mean`, `o2_sd`), but not in the T–S diagram.
- **Oxygen at depth:** the 1/4° biogeochemistry grid has only 3 cells in the ±20 km box at KGI and CI (6 at EI), so at KGI there is no oxygen below 773 m and at CI none below 1046 m. In the table, deeper species depths at those sites take oxygen from the deepest available level (KGI: BW37 920 m uses 773 m — Pm is not listed for KGI because it was never detected there; CI: Pm 1665 m uses 1046 m). This is the same fallback used in the model predictors (Section 2.5).
- **Oxygen units:** hydrographic literature (GO-SHIP/WOCE; e.g., Dotto et al. 2021 for this region [CONFIRM]) reports µmol kg⁻¹; older papers use mL L⁻¹; studies that use model output, including Copernicus, usually keep mmol m⁻³. We keep mmol m⁻³, the native model unit (1 mmol m⁻³ ≈ 0.973 µmol kg⁻¹ at ρ ≈ 1027.5 kg m⁻³). Natalie confirmed mmol m⁻³ (2026-11-18).
- **Colour scales:** temperature and salinity use the oce `temperature`/`salinity` palettes (cmocean-style). Oxygen uses viridis; I dropped oce's oxygen palette because its built-in red/yellow break points imply hypoxia/supersaturation thresholds that don't apply here.
- **Species-depth abbreviations:** the legend expands them, but they should match whatever you use in Table [species depths] (Section 2.6).
- **What the figure shows (for Results, not Methods):** at species depths, KGI is warmest (≈1.1–1.4 °C at 375–768 m), EI is intermediate (≈0.4–0.7 °C), and CI is coldest (≈0 °C) and most uniform. EI's deep values in the ±20 km box are noisier than in the ±40 km box. Oxygen falls from ~350 mmol m⁻³ at the surface to ~220 mmol m⁻³ below ~600 m at all sites.
- **Citation:** `oce` package (Kelley & Richards) — add to `references.bib` at the end.
