# 2.5 Environmental Data — methods draft

*Drafted from `05_environmental_data/01_download_copernicus.R`, `02_download_aviso_fsle.R`, `03_extract_aviso_fsle.R`, `04_extract_copernicus.R`, `05_compile_dataset.R`, `06_box_size_comparison.R`, `config.R`, and their outputs. Flags: `[INSERT]` = information not in the code; `[CONFIRM]` = value to verify. References: placeholders only, to be completed at the end.*

---

Environmental conditions at each site were characterized with daily output from the Copernicus Marine Service global ocean physics reanalysis (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030, GLORYS12V1, dataset version 202311; 1/12° horizontal resolution, 50 vertical levels) and global biogeochemistry hindcast (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_BGC_001_029, dataset version 202406; 1/4° resolution), both accessed 17–18 November 2026 [REF product DOIs]. From the physics reanalysis, we used potential temperature, salinity, zonal and meridional velocity, sea surface height, mixed layer depth, and sea-ice concentration (Section 2.3). Mixed layer depth is defined in this product as the depth at which density exceeds its value at 10 m by the amount equivalent to a 0.2 °C temperature decrease under local surface conditions. From the biogeochemistry hindcast, we used dissolved oxygen, chlorophyll-*a*, and net primary productivity. Following [COMPANION PAPER], mesoscale activity and fronts were described with backward-in-time finite-size Lyapunov exponents (FSLEs) and the orientations of the associated finite-size Lyapunov vectors (FSLVs) (d'Ovidio et al. 2004). These were obtained from the AVISO+ FSLE product, computed from DUACS delayed-time multi-satellite absolute geostrophic velocities (version DT2024; 1/25°, daily; accessed 17–18 November 2026) [REF AVISO+ acknowledgement].

Each variable was averaged daily over all ocean grid cells within ±20 km of each recorder (approximately 40 × 40 km). This area is larger than the distances over which the target species' echolocation clicks can be detected by a HARP: up to about 4 km for beaked whales (Zimmer et al. 2008, Hildebrand et al. 2015), [INSERT range, with reference] for killer whales and long-finned pilot whales, and up to about 20 km for sperm whales, which at these latitudes are adult males [REF]. It therefore includes the water around any detected animal. The box was also large enough to contain several grid cells of the coarser 1/4° biogeochemistry product (one cell spans ~28 km in latitude), whereas a ±10 km box would contain at most one. The ±20 km box contained 32–45 physics cells (EI 32, KGI 36, CI 45) and 3–6 biogeochemistry cells (EI 6, KGI 3, CI 3) at the surface. Water-column variables were taken at the surface (0.5 m) and at the model level nearest each species' typical foraging depth(s) (Table [INSERT]; Section 2.6). Where no biogeochemistry grid cell within the box reached that level, the deepest level with water was used (oxygen at KGI: 773 m in place of 920 and 1665 m; at CI: 1046 m in place of 1665 m). Deep levels covered only part of each box because the shelf slope runs through it, so deep box means represent only the cells deep enough to reach that level.

Eddy kinetic energy (EKE) was calculated as ½(*u*′² + *v*′²), where *u*′ and *v*′ are the daily box-mean velocity components minus their 10-year means at that site and depth. Within-box variability of the flow was calculated as ½(MAD*ᵤ*² + MAD*ᵥ*²), where MAD is the median absolute deviation of each velocity component across the grid cells of the box on that day. Mean FSLE included grid cells with an FSLE of zero, where the particle-separation threshold was not reached within the integration time. These zeros are valid values, not missing data. FSLV orientation is axial (orientations 180° apart describe the same front), so it was averaged by doubling the angles, taking the circular mean, and halving the result, using only the cells with nonzero FSLE (on average 87–95% of the box per day). To represent delayed responses to productivity, we also calculated lagged chlorophyll-*a* and primary productivity at the surface as the mean of the 30-day window ending 1–6 months before each day.

All variables were extracted for each site's deployment years ± 5 years (EI 2009–2019, KGI 2010–2021, CI 2011–2021). This 10-year record provided the climatologies used to deseason predictors (Section 2.6) and the reference mean flow for EKE, and it allowed lagged predictors to be calculated for the first months of each deployment. No predictor had missing values on any recording day.
