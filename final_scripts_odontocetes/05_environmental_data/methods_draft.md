# 2.5 Environmental Data — methods draft

*Drafted from `05_environmental_data/01_download_copernicus.R`, `02_download_aviso_fsle.R`, `03_extract_aviso_fsle.R`, `04_extract_copernicus.R`, `05_compile_dataset.R`, `config.R`, and their outputs. Revised 2026-11-19 for depth layers and the final predictor set.*

---

Environmental conditions at each site were characterized with daily output from the Copernicus Marine Service global ocean physics reanalysis (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030, GLORYS12V1, dataset version 202311; 1/12° horizontal resolution, 50 vertical levels) and global biogeochemistry hindcast (GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_BGC_001_029, dataset version 202406; 1/4° resolution), both accessed 17–18 November 2026 [REF product DOIs]. From the physics reanalysis, we used potential temperature, zonal and meridional velocity, mixed layer depth, and sea-ice concentration (Section 2.3). Mixed layer depth is defined in this product as the depth at which density exceeds its value at 10 m by the amount equivalent to a 0.2 °C temperature decrease under local surface conditions. From the biogeochemistry hindcast, we used chlorophyll-*a*. Following [COMPANION PAPER], fronts and surface mesoscale stirring were described with backward-in-time finite-size Lyapunov exponents (FSLEs) and the orientations of the associated finite-size Lyapunov vectors (FSLVs) (d'Ovidio et al. 2004), from the AVISO+ FSLE product computed from DUACS delayed-time multi-satellite absolute geostrophic velocities (version DT2024; 1/25°, daily; accessed 17–18 November 2026) [REF AVISO+ acknowledgement].

Each variable was averaged daily over all ocean grid cells within ±20 km of each recorder (approximately 40 × 40 km). This area is larger than the distances over which the target species' echolocation clicks can be detected by a HARP: up to about 4 km for beaked whales (Zimmer et al. 2008, Hildebrand et al. 2015), [INSERT range, with reference] for killer whales and long-finned pilot whales, and up to about 20 km for sperm whales, which at these latitudes are adult males [REF]. It therefore includes the water around any detected animal. The box was also large enough to contain several grid cells of the coarser 1/4° biogeochemistry product (one cell spans ~28 km in latitude), whereas a ±10 km box would contain at most one. The ±20 km box contained 32–45 physics cells (EI 32, KGI 36, CI 45) and 3–6 biogeochemistry cells (EI 6, KGI 3, CI 3) at the surface.

Water-column variables were averaged over three depth layers: surface (0–100 m), mid-water (100–500 m), and deep (500–1000 m) (Section 2.2; Supplement S[INSERT]). Each layer mean was weighted by thickness: each model level represents the depth interval between the midpoints to its neighbouring levels, and its weight is the overlap of that interval with the layer. Only grid cells with water at a level contributed, so deep-layer means represent the parts of the box deep enough to reach them. Eddy kinetic energy (EKE) was calculated at each model level as ½(*u*′² + *v*′²), where *u*′ and *v*′ are the daily box-mean velocity components minus their 10-year means at that site and level, and then averaged over the mid-water and deep layers in the same way. Because a box mean over fewer grid cells averages out less of the eddy variability, absolute EKE values are not directly comparable between layers or sites (e.g., at EI, deep-layer EKE exceeded surface EKE); within a site and layer, its variation over time is comparable.

Mean FSLE included grid cells with an FSLE of zero, where the particle-separation threshold was not reached within the integration time; these zeros are valid values, not missing data. FSLV orientation is axial (orientations 180° apart describe the same front), so it was averaged by doubling the angles, taking the circular mean, and halving the result, using only the cells with nonzero FSLE (on average 87–95% of the box per day). To represent the delayed response of odontocete prey to phytoplankton production, surface chlorophyll-*a* was averaged over the 30-day window ending 3 months before each day.

All variables were extracted for each site's deployment years ± 5 years (EI 2009–2019, KGI 2010–2021, CI 2011–2021). This 10-year record provided the climatologies used to deseason predictors (Section 2.6) and the reference mean flow for EKE, and it allowed lagged chlorophyll-*a* to be calculated for the first months of each deployment. No predictor had missing values on any recording day.
